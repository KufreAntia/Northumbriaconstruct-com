Bridge the Gap

The project that led to the creation of Northumbria Construct.

Project overview

Developed during the Association for Project Management (APM) Project Management Challenge, the project investigated why many students struggled to engage effectively with the construction industry despite the wealth of opportunities available through universities, employers and professional institutions.

Using structured project management methodologies, the project combined research, stakeholder engagement, industry interviews, storytelling and educational outreach to better understand the barriers preventing meaningful participation.

The findings demonstrated that the primary challenge was not the absence of opportunity, but the lack of visibility, understanding and accessibility.

Recognising that these challenges extended beyond the competition itself, the project team transformed Bridge the Gap into a continuing programme of professional engagement, leading to the establishment of Northumbria Construct.

Project objectives

  • Investigate barriers affecting student engagement with industry.
  • Strengthen collaboration between academia and the built environment.
  • Improve awareness of professional pathways and career opportunities.
  • Encourage participation in professional institutions, volunteering and networking.
  • Deliver sustainable educational benefits beyond the project.

Learning that shaped the organisation

The project found that opportunities needed greater visibility, understanding and accessibility. Its transition into Northumbria Construct carried that learning into a continuing engagement programme.

Key activities

  • Research and stakeholder surveys
  • Industry interviews and professional storytelling
  • Educational seminars and outreach activities
  • University, institution and industry collaboration
  • Innovative stakeholder engagement approaches
  • Transition planning for long-term sustainability

Project outcomes

  • Better understanding of engagement challenges
  • Evidence-based recommendations
  • Innovative engagement frameworks
  • A sustainable programme of professional engagement
  • Demonstrated benefit realisation through transition

Project · Newcastle Hub

Oxford-Style Debate

A structured debate that turned discussion into practical recommendations and new opportunities.

Debate motion
“Universities Are Failing to Prepare Graduates for Industry.”

Project overview

The project brought together participants, academics and industry professionals to examine the responsibilities of universities, individuals, employers and professional institutions in preparing graduates for professional practice.

Audience feedback and recommendations were analysed and compiled into a formal report presented to stakeholders within Northumbria University Experiential Learning Team and the Chartered Institute of Building (CIOB).

Recommendations included clearer communication around opportunities and improved signposting of Personal Protective Equipment availability for construction site visits. These actions contributed to increased participation in subsequent CIOB site visits.

Planning, delivery and learning

The managed engagement brought together participants, academics and industry professionals, collected audience feedback and presented recommendations to stakeholders. The delivery experience demonstrated the value of clearer opportunity communication and PPE signposting.

Version 1 of NorCon Projects was tested through the project, including briefs, activity planning, Gantt charts, progress tracking, stakeholder coordination, RACI matrices, dashboards and reporting. Explore its applications.

Project · Newcastle Hub

Supporting the Gateshead engagement day.

CIOB North East Tomorrow’s Leaders Professional Engagement Day · Gateshead College · 7 October 2026

Northumbria Construct contributed to the planning, coordination and delivery of the professional engagement day involving CIOB North East Tomorrow’s Leaders and Gateshead College.

Coordinating participation

Our contribution covered stakeholder coordination, volunteer mobilisation, project planning, activity preparation and resource coordination. NorCon Projects was used to support the work, alongside the BRIC construction careers activity.

Contributions and learning

The engagement involved CIOB Tomorrow’s Leaders, Gateshead College, Northumbria Construct and individual volunteers. Northumbria Construct’s contribution forms part of that collective delivery.

Delivery and learning

Callum O’Connor led Northumbria Construct’s volunteer team, alongside Vemula Sai Praneeth, Finn Mulholland, Aditya Shetty, Mayuri Shinde and Collins Tamunotonye. Activities drew on the CIOB Think Construction Toolkit and piloted BRIC with approximately 40 students across eight teams.

The pilot identified opportunities to simplify BRIC instructions and refine gameplay. Volunteers put leadership, communication, teamwork and stakeholder engagement into practice; the experience reinforced the value of participatory learning and accessible professional networks.

Testing project governance in practice

Version 2 of NorCon Projects supported the contribution, building on the debate application with baselines and governance, risk and issue management, change control, benefits and value management, sustainability tracking and enhanced reporting.
